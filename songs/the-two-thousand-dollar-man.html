Two figures in the ballad

The song follows two fictional men from coastal Georgia. A probate inventory values an enslaved millwright in Chatham County at two thousand dollars. When the estate’s debts come due, his family is sold apart. Across the Ogeechee in Bryan County, a tenant farmer enlists as a Confederate private. The inventory records a price imposed on a person held as property. The soldier’s pay measures what the army offers him for his service.

Savannah and the Ogeechee

On December 13, 1864, Union troops captured Fort McAllister on the Ogeechee River, opening a supply route for Sherman’s army as it approached Savannah. The city fell on December 21. The song uses these events to bring the millwright’s search and the soldier’s letters into the same winter.

Family separation and freedom

A firsthand account of the March 1859 slave auction in Savannah records buyers breaking up families. It provides historical context for the fictional sale in the song. The millwright’s spring journey to Macon takes up a question emancipation left unresolved for separated families: where to begin looking when the people who sold them controlled the records of where they went.
