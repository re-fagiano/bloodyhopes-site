Historical Context

Three unrelated disasters, four centuries apart, share the same shape. Ferdinand Magellan, leading the Spanish expedition to the East Indies, was killed by a poisoned arrow at the Battle of Mactan in the Philippines on April 27, 1521 — struck down leading a landing party far from the ships that could have supported him. On June 25, 1876, Lieutenant Colonel George Custer led some 210 men of the U.S. Seventh Cavalry into the Battle of the Little Bighorn, cut off from reinforcement and annihilated. And on May 25, 1928, the airship Italia crashed onto Arctic ice; its survivors sheltered for weeks in a hand-sewn crimson shelter that became known as the Red Tent, sending SOS signals that took two full weeks to even be received, while Roald Amundsen himself died searching for them by seaplane.

Each event turns on the same failure: a message that needed to travel faster than it could, help that was sent but arrived too late, too far, or not at all.

The Man Sent to Say It's Too Late

"Send the Italian" builds its narrative around a recurring, half-legendary figure who appears at the edge of all three stories — the messenger, scout, or radio operator dispatched to bring word or bring help, who either does not arrive in time or arrives only to confirm that the worst has already happened. It's a deliberately anachronistic device: a single narrative thread pulled taut across the Philippines in 1521, Montana in 1876, and the Arctic ice in 1928, asking the same question in three settings four hundred years apart — what does it cost to be the one sent too late, and to know it before you get there.

Lyrics

The captain called for volunteers.
The Italian asked:
“Is there a message?”

Five ships sailed out from Seville,
silk and scripture down below.
Magellan spoke of crowns and kingdoms,
men spoke softly of the gold.

One ship wrecked, one turned for home,
one burned, one fell in chains.
Victoria reached the harbour
with eighteen ghosts and cloves.

Pigafetta stepped ashore still writing,
with the dead men in his pen.
“The food was poor,” he noted.
“Remarkable voyage, again.”

Send the Italian with the message,
send him while the guns begin.
Give him paper, give him orders,
give him one good road out in.

He is not retreating,
that is not the case at all.
He is carrying vital news
when the brave men make their fall.

Custer watched the valley moving,
dust and lodges in the heat.
Someone said, “We need the packs, sir.”
Someone said, “We must be quick.”

“Martini, take this note to Benteen.
Ride until the horses foam.”
John folded it inside his jacket
and wisely rode alone.

At dusk the field lay quiet,
and the flags were lying flat.
Martini reached Benteen’s column.
No one sent him back.

Send the Italian with the message,
send him while the guns begin.
Give him paper, give him orders,
give him one good road out in.

He is not retreating,
he is moving with dispatch.
History may keep the medals.
He will keep his hat.

Over Arctic ice Italia broke,
red tent in the snow.
Biagi warmed the little radio
and would not let it go.

Frozen hands and fading batteries,
static in the polar night,
he tapped until a stranger heard him
through the crackle and the white.

“Biagi, how did you endure it?”
He said, “The set required care.”
Then he sent another signal.
Someone, somewhere, heard it there.

Send the Italian with the message,
wire, a saddle, ink, or flame.
Empires crack and captains thunder,
but the message still gets through.

He is not retreating.
Write it clearly in the log:
“Absent from the charge, sir,
on essential service.”
