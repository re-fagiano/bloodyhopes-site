Historical Context

The Risorgimento grew from secret societies into a two-decades-long nationalist campaign that transformed a fragmented peninsula of kingdoms, duchies, and foreign-occupied territories into the unified Kingdom of Italy. Three very different men drove it forward: Giuseppe Mazzini, whose revolutionary writing and secret societies inspired the movement's ideology; Count Camillo di Cavour, whose diplomacy and political alliances secured foreign support and legitimacy; and Giuseppe Garibaldi, whose volunteer "Redshirts" won the decisive military campaigns, most famously the conquest of Sicily and Naples in 1860.

The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in 1861, with unification substantially completed by 1870-71 with the addition of Rome and the Veneto — the culmination of a movement that had started decades earlier as scattered, often-crushed revolutionary conspiracies.

A Declaration Before a Fact

For most of the Risorgimento's history, "Italy" was an aspiration contradicted by the map: a peninsula divided between Austrian territory, the Papal States, the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, and various smaller states, none of which recognized the nationalist claim that they were secretly one country waiting to be assembled. The song's title captures that gap between conviction and reality — a declaration made loudly and repeatedly for years before it became true, by people who had every practical reason to doubt it would ever happen and said it anyway.
