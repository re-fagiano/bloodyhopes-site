Historical Context

Frederick II became widely known in later Prussian and German memory as “Der Alte Fritz” (“Old Fritz”). The affectionate image grew especially powerful after his reign and should not be mistaken for a universal opinion among the soldiers who fought his wars. His campaigns, above all the Seven Years' War, established Prussia as a major European military power and helped create a durable public cult around his name.

Frederick's army included substantial numbers of foreigners, prisoners, deserters, and men recruited or pressed beyond the Prussian heartland. Estimates vary by date and by what historians count as “foreign,” so a fixed majority should not be asserted without a specified roster or study. The larger point remains: service under Frederick did not imply personal loyalty to him.

Two Toasts, One Army

“Cheers to Fritz, and to hell with Fritz” is the ballad's fictional compression of those conflicting experiences. It is historically plausible that admiration, obedience, resentment, and coercion coexisted in Frederick's army, but the refrain is not a transcript of a documented regiment on the night before battle.
