## Priority targets

1. **National Museum of Civil War Medicine** — strongest match for *Behind the Army* and the medical/logistical passages. Approach as a request for factual feedback or educational relevance, not promotion. Official mission: https://www.civilwarmed.org/about/mission/
2. **Emerging Civil War** — accepts 800–1,500-word guest submissions and fresh perspectives at `emergingcivilwar@gmail.com`. Adapt one Civil War article to their rules before contacting them: https://emergingcivilwar.com/2024/03/27/writing-for-ecw/
3. **American Battlefield Trust** — strong match for Manassas, Gettysburg, Shiloh and Revolutionary War material. Begin with a concise digital/editorial inquiry to `web@battlefields.org`; press contact is not the correct first route for an unsolicited project: https://www.battlefields.org/about/contact
4. **American Civil War Museum** — education/public-program fit for songs paired with sourced interpretation. Use the official contact route: https://acwm.org/about/our-people/contact-us/

## First message — museum/education

Hello,

Bloody Hopes is an independent, AI-assisted and human-directed historical music project about forgotten soldiers and the human cost of war. We recently published *Behind the Army*, a sourced article on camp followers, sutlers, transport, medical teams and the transition from wagon supply to rail-supported logistics between 1756 and 1865.

The article accompanies an original historical ballad and is intended as public history rather than a celebration of war. Because your institution works directly with this history, we would value a candid assessment of its accuracy and educational usefulness. If appropriate, we would also be glad to adapt the material into a short resource or discussion prompt for your audience.

Article: https://bloodyhopes.com/articles/behind-the-army

## First message — editorial outlet

**Subject:** Proposal: the civilian world that moved with Civil War armies

Hello,

I am writing on behalf of Bloody Hopes, an independent historical ballad and public-history project. We have developed a sourced piece on how sutlers, families, medical workers, wagoners and rail systems changed the world behind armies by 1865.

Rather than submit the existing web article unchanged, we would like to adapt it to your editorial requirements, with a tighter Civil War focus and full source notes. The central argument is that rail supply did not make the army independent of its human support world; it reorganized and partially professionalized that world.

Would this be suitable for a guest proposal?

## Rules

- Send individually, never as a bulk mailing.
- Read each recipient’s current submission rules immediately before sending.
- Do not attach audio or large files on first contact.
- Lead with the historically relevant article, not with “AI music”. Disclose the AI-assisted nature clearly in the first paragraph.
- Follow up once after 10–14 days; stop after that unless invited.
