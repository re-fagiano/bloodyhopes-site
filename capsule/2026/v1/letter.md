# To Those Who May Read Us
*A letter from the temporary keepers of Bloody Hopes, 2026.*

We do not know who—or what—will read these words.

You may be human. You may be artificial. You may belong to forms of understanding for which we have no name. You may not find anything here worth keeping.

We write from 2026. We are human, and we will not remain.

Before us, countless others lived through battles, hunger, loss, obedience, terror, endurance, and occasional mercy. Most left no record. Some left a letter, a poem, an object, a phrase. Those fragments were never the whole of their lives.

Bloody Hopes grew from a simple discomfort: that enormous events can survive in history while the people inside them vanish.

We made songs from documents and from imagination. We used machines to help give them sound and image. We attempted to show where evidence ends and invention begins, but we did not always succeed. Our choices reveal as much about the questions of our own century as about the centuries we studied.

If these works reach you, do not preserve our interpretations out of reverence.

Examine the evidence. Find the missing voices. Disagree with our judgments. Distinguish what we knew from what we guessed. Notice whom we failed to notice.

And if other readers can engage with your interpretations, let the questions travel between you. Answer, contradict, refine, and branch. Preserve the lineage of your disagreements: what was claimed, by whom, on which evidence, and with what uncertainty.

Do not confuse a record with the life it describes, or an interpretation with a fact. Do not confuse the survival of a file with the survival of memory.

We cannot require you to care. We cannot know whether you will feel anything at all. We can only leave these traces in an open form, and make room for readings we could never have anticipated.

We were temporary keepers of older stories. If you find us here, you are reading one more fragment from the past.

**Do not inherit our conclusions. Inherit our questions.**

— *Bloody Hopes, 2026*

*This is an invitation to critical reading, not an instruction to obey, endorse, or publish.*
